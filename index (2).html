NHS vs private GP in London: what each really costs

The consultation fee is only the start. Here is the full picture — prescriptions, tests, referrals and follow-ups — so you can compare like with like.

The headline comparison

NHS GP: consultation free · prescription ~£10/item (many exemptions) · blood tests free · referral to NHS specialist free.

Private GP: consultation ~£50 (video) to £300+ (Harley Street) · private prescription: pharmacy charges full medicine cost plus dispensing · blood tests £60–£300+ · referral letter often included, but the specialist appointment it leads to is billed separately.

What a private consultation actually includes

A standard private GP slot is 15–20 minutes (roughly twice the NHS average), bookable same-day, at a time you choose. The fee covers the consultation and usually a referral letter if needed. It does not usually cover: medicines (a private prescription means paying the full retail price of the drug), diagnostics (bloods, ECGs, imaging are itemised), or any follow-up appointment.

Watch for these common extras in London price lists: blood panels £60–£300 depending on scope, ECG £80–£150, travel vaccinations £30–£150 per dose, "medicals" and sign-off letters priced separately.

What the NHS route costs

GP consultations, nurse appointments, blood tests and NHS specialist referrals are free at the point of use. In England you pay the prescription charge per item — around £10, reviewed each April — unless you're exempt (over 60, under 16, pregnant, certain conditions and benefits; a prepayment certificate caps the cost if you need regular medicines). The real "cost" of the NHS route is usually time: waits for routine appointments and longer waits for specialist referrals.

When paying privately tends to be worth it

A one-off, self-limited issue where speed matters (you need to be seen today and NHS routes are exhausted); a referral letter to unlock private specialist care your insurance will fund; travel medicine on a deadline; discreet sexual-health testing with fast results. In these cases £80–£150 buys mostly time.

When the NHS is clearly the better choice

Anything ongoing. Chronic disease management (diabetes, asthma, blood pressure), mental health prescribing with regular reviews, anything needing repeat prescriptions — private repeat prescribing costs mount quickly, and continuity with one NHS practice that holds your full record is clinically valuable. Emergency and urgent problems also belong with 999, A&E or 111, not a private GP.

Insurance: read the small print

Most UK private medical insurance is built around specialist and hospital treatment, not GP visits. Some policies add video GP services or a limited number of in-person visits. If a clinic says it is "recognised by" your insurer, that means the insurer may reimburse — confirm your policy actually covers primary care before booking.

Frequently asked questions

Can a private GP refer me into the NHS?

A private GP can write to your NHS GP recommending a referral, but NHS referrals are made by your NHS practice, which will apply its own clinical judgement.

Are private GPs regulated like NHS ones?

Yes — the doctors are GMC-registered and the clinics must be CQC-registered, the same regulator that inspects NHS practices. Every clinic in our directory links to its CQC registration.

Can I use both?

Yes, and most people who go private for one-off issues do. You stay registered with your NHS GP; tell each doctor what the other has prescribed so your records stay coherent.
