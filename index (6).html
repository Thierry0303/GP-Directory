GP catchment areas in London, explained

Every NHS GP practice defines a geographic boundary for its patients — but the rules are more flexible than most Londoners realise. Here's how boundaries actually work, and how to use the flexibility.

Inner and outer boundaries

Practices typically define an inner boundary (the core catchment — anyone living here can register, and gets the full service including home visits when clinically needed) and often an outer boundary: if you're already registered and move into this ring, the practice may keep you on its list rather than forcing a switch. Boundaries are set by each practice and agreed with its commissioner; in dense inner London they can be just a few streets wide.

Registering out-of-area: the commuter option

Since 2015, practices in England can register patients who live outside their boundary, at the practice's discretion, under national out-of-area arrangements. The trade-off: the practice has no obligation to visit you at home; if you need care at home, it's arranged through a service near where you live. This suits commuters who'd rather see a GP near work, and it's entirely legitimate — but the practice can say no, and many oversubscribed London practices do.

Moving house

Moved within the boundary? Just update your address. Moved outside it? The practice chooses: keep you (as an outer-boundary or out-of-area patient) or ask you to re-register locally. If you must switch, registration is quick and your records transfer automatically — see how to switch GP in London.

Students and temporary residents

Students register fully at their term-time address — no need to stay registered at home. Anyone staying somewhere between 24 hours and 3 months can be seen as a temporary resident at a local practice without leaving their home practice's list.

How to check a practice's boundary

Most practices publish a boundary map on their website; the NHS online registration service checks your postcode automatically when you apply. If you're borderline, phone and ask — practices have discretion at the margins, especially for existing patients' family members.

Refused because of your address?

Living outside the catchment is one of the few lawful reasons a practice can refuse registration. But refusal for lack of proof of address is not — you don't need documents to prove where you live. If your whole area's practices have closed lists, your Integrated Care Board has a duty to find you a practice: contact NHS England's customer contact centre and ask about "allocation to a GP practice".

Frequently asked questions

Can a practice remove me for moving away?

Yes, if you move outside its boundary a practice may write to you asking you to register elsewhere, usually giving around 30 days. It cannot remove you without warning except in exceptional circumstances.

Can family members at the same address be split across practices?

Yes — registration is individual. Practices often accommodate family members of existing patients, but they aren't obliged to.
